# ASO and store listings

Never change a live listing during research. Measure, write the plan, get approval, then change one thing at a time so you can read the effect.

## 1. Measure today (per store, per market)

- Current title, subtitle or short description, keyword field, screenshots, rating count and average, last update.
- Rank for 20-40 candidate phrases per market (top 200 App Store, top 30 Play is a practical depth). Write absolute dates next to every reading.
- Store console data: impressions, product page views, conversion to install, top search terms that already bring installs.

## 2. Find demand

- Volume per market and language, measured in each language. Never translate a keyword list.
- Demand often sits in a **cluster** ("how well do you know me"), not in your product name. Put brand + cluster phrase in the subtitle.
- For each phrase record: volume, top 10 competitors, their rating counts. A niche where the top 10 has small rating counts (single or low double digits) is winnable; broad phrases owned by apps with thousands of ratings are not, until you have ratings.

## 3. Structure: one app, several listings

- Separate "clone" apps per feature or game are a textbook rejection under store rules on repetitive content and spam, and they split your ratings.
- Use the store tools instead: Custom Product Pages and In-App Events (App Store), Custom Store Listings and promotional content (Google Play). One listing per audience or feature, each with its own screenshots and copy, each a separate ad destination.

## 4. Listing copy

- Title: brand + the strongest cluster phrase, within the store limit. Check the current character limits in the official docs every time; they change.
- App Store keyword field: comma-separated, no spaces, no repeats of words already in the title, no competitor brand names.
- Play: the full description is indexed; write for people first, repeat the core phrase naturally 3-5 times.
- Write natively per language (see `positioning-and-copy`), run the AI-tell pass.

## 5. Screenshots

- The first two frames decide. Frame 1: the outcome or the "wow" moment with a 3-6 word caption. Frame 2: how it works in one picture.
- Real UI only. Captions large enough to read in search results on a small phone.
- Variants per listing and per language; test with the store's built-in A/B tools where available.

## 6. Ratings

- Ask at a moment of success (after a finished session, never after an error), using the native review prompt.
- Track rating count per market; it unlocks broad phrases more than any copy change.

## 7. Plan and decisions

Single HTML plan: verdict, today, demand, competitors, proposed listing per store and market, screenshot list, measurement plan (which number, read when), and a **To decide** list with default answers. Nothing changes in the stores before approval.

## Done when

Rankings and console numbers are recorded with dates, every proposed phrase has measured volume and competitor rating counts, the plan is approved, and after a change the same phrases are re-measured on a scheduled date.
